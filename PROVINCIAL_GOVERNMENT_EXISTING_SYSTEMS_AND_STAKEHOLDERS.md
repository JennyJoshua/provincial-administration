## Papua New Guinea Provincial Administration Intelligence and Performance System (PAIPS)

**Handover reference:** PNG-PAIPS-RESEARCH-002  
**Previous report:** `PROVINCIAL_GOVERNMENT_RESEARCH_MASTER.md`  
**Research date:** 23 October 2026  
**Status:** Research and verification only. No software has been built, no repository files modified, no government approval claimed, and no pilot partner selected.

---

## 1. Executive summary

This verification research finds that **DPLGA already operates a formal provincial performance-reporting process**, but the available public evidence does **not** establish that it operates a mature, province-wide digital performance-management platform. DPLGA’s current organisational structure assigns its Corporate Planning and Performance Management Reporting branch to develop and institutionalise provincial performance-reporting systems, while a separate Strategic Policy and Digital Integration Division is tasked with developing digital platforms and tools for data collection, performance monitoring, and inter-governmental communication. This indicates an active institutional mandate, but not proof of a deployed, fully operational system. [21]

The strongest current evidence is that provinces submit **Annual Provincial Performance Reports (APPRs)** and Section 114/119 reports to DPLGA through a formal handover and compilation process. In 2025, DPLGA reported that 14 of 20 provinces had submitted their 2024 APPRs; reports are then compiled by DPLGA officers and provided to the Minister for tabling in Parliament. [43] Enga formally presented its 2023 and 2024 APPRs in June 2025, and Western Highlands presented its 2024 Section 119 report in August 2025. [44][52]

IFMS is confirmed as PNG’s mandated accounting, budgeting, and financial-reporting system for national departments, statutory authorities, and provincial and local-level governments. It provides financial transactions, budgeting, payments, revenue and grant accounting, expenditure processing, and financial reporting. It requires internet connectivity, and the Department of Finance explicitly identifies connectivity interruptions and slow speeds outside Port Moresby as a material operational concern. [16]

The **smallest evidence-supported capability gap** is therefore not “a provincial dashboard” in general. It is a narrow operational layer that can help provinces and DPLGA manage the existing APPR/Section 119 workflow: collect departmental inputs, attach evidence, verify submissions, track deadlines and overdue items, consolidate a draft report, and manage corrective actions. Whether this should be built as a new tool, an extension of a DPLGA system, or an integration layer remains unresolved because DPLGA’s current digital-platform status is not publicly documented. [21][43][52]

**Recommended outcome: Option D — conduct interviews and validation before deciding, with a working assumption that the eventual answer is likely to be a small integration layer rather than a new standalone platform.**

## 2. Research scope and methodology

### Scope

This report verifies the current status of systems and processes relevant to provincial administration reporting in PNG. It focuses on:

- DPLGA systems and reporting processes.
- IFMS capabilities and integration uncertainties.
- Other relevant national systems.
- Three provincial examples: Enga, Western Highlands, and West New Britain.
- Government stakeholders and decision authority.
- A capability matrix and gap classification.
- Five solution options.
- Adoption, procurement, and commercial feasibility.

### Evidence hierarchy

Priority was given to:

1. Current official PNG government sources, especially DPLGA and Department of Finance.
2. Official legislation, plans, reports, and implementation documents.
3. Official provincial administration publications.
4. Credible development-partner and policy sources.
5. Credible journalism or provincial news for corroboration.

### Evidence rules applied

The research distinguishes between:

- **Announced:** stated as an intention or responsibility.
- **Approved / funded:** supported by an approval or funding decision.
- **Under development:** actively being built or configured.
- **Piloted:** tested in a limited setting.
- **Deployed / operational:** available for intended users.
- **Independently evaluated:** assessed by an external assurance or evaluation body.

A website description is treated as evidence of a mandate or intended function, not proof of full operational maturity. Missing public information is recorded as unknown, not as proof that a system does not exist.

### Limitations

No interviews were conducted. No access to internal DPLGA systems, IFMS configurations, provincial reporting templates, or non-public procurement records was available. The report therefore cannot verify system usage, data quality, user adoption, or whether DPLGA has an internal platform not described publicly.

## 3. Current DPLGA systems and reporting processes

### DPLGA’s formal mandate

DPLGA describes itself as responsible for overseeing development and implementation of policies, programmes, and initiatives relating to provincial and local-level government administration. It coordinates with provinces and LLGs, provides technical assistance and capacity building, and facilitates intergovernmental communication. [21]

Its **Corporate Planning and Performance Management Reporting** branch is specifically responsible for:

- Formulating, coordinating, and reviewing DPLGA corporate plans and annual activity plans.
- Driving institutional performance monitoring, evaluation, and reporting.
- Developing and institutionalising provincial performance-reporting systems for provinces, districts, cities, LLGs, and special-purpose authorities.
- Supporting performance-data collection, analysis, and dissemination.
- Facilitating internal and external performance reviews.
- Administering the Executive Performance Management System. [21]

This is clear evidence of an **existing institutional responsibility**, but the wording—“develop and institutionalise”—does not by itself establish that a complete digital system is already operating across all provinces. [21]

### Section 114 and Section 119 reporting

DPLGA’s Corporate Planning and Reporting Branch facilitates:

- **Section 114 reports:** financial affairs reporting by provincial governments and LLGs.
- **Section 119 reports:** governance-affairs reporting by provincial governments and LLGs. [21]

These are established statutory reporting processes, not merely proposed concepts. DPLGA’s 2026 publication, *The Local Voice*, states that APPRs are mandated under Section 187(j) of the National Constitution and are essential for parliamentary reporting under Section 119 of the Organic Law on Provincial Governments and Local-Level Governments. [43]

### Current APPR process

The available evidence describes the following process:

1. Provincial administrations prepare Annual Provincial Performance Reports.
2. Reports are formally handed to DPLGA, often through a ceremony involving the Provincial Administrator and DPLGA Secretary or senior officers.
3. DPLGA officers compile submitted reports.
4. Compiled reports are provided to the Minister.
5. The Minister tables the reports in Parliament. [43][52]

This is a **formal, operating reporting process**, but the public evidence does not establish whether it is supported by a shared electronic workflow, structured database, automated deadline tracking, evidence repository, or dashboard.

### Current submission status

DPLGA reported in 2025 that, of 20 provinces, **14 had submitted their 2024 APPRs**. The listed provinces included East New Britain, Enga, Gulf, Jiwaka, Madang, Manus, Milne Bay, New Ireland, Oro, Southern Highlands, Simbu, West New Britain, West Sepik, and West Highlands. [43]

This is important evidence:

- It confirms that APPR reporting is active.
- It confirms that DPLGA tracks submission at province level.
- It also indicates incomplete submission: six of 20 provinces had not submitted by the time of the reported status.
- It does not establish whether the tracking is manual, spreadsheet-based, or digital.

### PLLSMA

The Provincial and Local-Level Services Monitoring Authority (PLLSMA) is established under Section 110 of the Organic Law on Provincial and Local-Level Government Affairs. DPLGA describes its mandate as developing and monitoring implementation of national policies and decisions at sub-national government level, establishing minimum development standards, assisting the Auditor-General with performance audits, assessing provincial and local-government effectiveness and efficiency, and making recommendations to the Minister. [21]

PLLSMA is therefore a governance and monitoring institution, but the available public evidence does not identify a named operational digital monitoring platform, dashboard, or public reporting portal operated by PLLSMA.

### DPLGA digital-integration function

DPLGA’s Strategic Policy and Digital Integration Division includes a **Special Functions and Digital Integrations** unit responsible for:

- Developing and implementing digital platforms and tools for data collection, performance monitoring, and inter-governmental communication.
- Facilitating ICT-system integration.
- Providing technical guidance, training, and support on digital solutions for provincial and LLG administrations.
- Collaborating with government agencies, development partners, and the private sector.
- Monitoring and evaluating digital initiatives. [21]

This is the most important DPLGA finding for PAIPS: DPLGA already claims responsibility for exactly the category of capability PAIPS would address. Any proposal should therefore begin with DPLGA, not with a province alone, to avoid duplication.

## 4. Current IFMS capabilities and integration uncertainties

### Confirmed IFMS functions

The Department of Finance describes IFMS as the Government of PNG’s designated accounting and finance system, intended to replace the legacy PNG Government Accounting System (PGAS). Its use is compulsory for national departments, statutory authorities, and provincial and local-level governments. [16]

IFMS provides:

- Budgeting.
- Accounting for revenue, grants, and expenditure.
- Payment processing.
- Financial reporting.
- Real-time accounting and financial data.
- Centralised control and auditability.
- Separate processing environments for national, sub-national, and statutory-authority operations. [16]

### Provincial rollout

The Department of Finance states that East New Britain was the initial sub-national pilot in 2017, followed by Central and Oro provinces in 2018. During 2018, IFMS was rolled out to all 20 provincial finance offices and 83 district finance offices—103 sites across 20 provinces, excluding NCDC and Bougainville. The Department further planned rollout to remaining provincial and local-level governments, district development authorities, and provincial health authorities. [16]

This establishes that IFMS is not merely proposed: it has been deployed to provincial and district finance offices. However, deployment to finance offices does not prove that every provincial department, manager, or operational unit uses IFMS directly, nor that non-financial performance data is captured in IFMS.

### IFMS management information

The Department states that IFMS is intended to provide more accurate, timely, real-time financial information to support decision-making, accountability, transparency, and faster financial auditing and reporting. [16]

However, the public IFMS project page does not document:

- A provincial administrator dashboard.
- Departmental work-plan tracking.
- Service-delivery indicators.
- Project-milestone tracking.
- Evidence attachment for non-financial activities.
- Supervisor verification workflows.
- Management-action registers.
- Public APIs or scheduled extracts.
- Export or redistribution permissions.

These remain **unresolved** rather than absent.

### IFMS connectivity constraint

The Department of Finance explicitly states that IFMS is a real-time system requiring internet connectivity and adequate bandwidth. It identifies frequent interruptions and slow internet speeds, particularly outside Port Moresby, as a major concern that can make IFMS unusable. [16]

This is directly relevant to PAIPS: any operational reporting layer must not assume continuous connectivity, and it should not require real-time IFMS access for frontline reporting.

## 6. Provincial examples

### Enga Provincial Administration

Enga provides the strongest current evidence of active APPR participation. Its administration formally presented Annual Provincial Performance Reports for 2023 and 2024 to DPLGA in June 2025. The report preparation involved intensive training, data collection from Minimum Priority Activities and non-MPA sectors, and efforts to verify critical data. The provincial administration reported difficulties including limited availability and verification of data, operational pressures, logistical hurdles, and delays in retrieving the 2023 report. [52]

Enga’s Monitoring and Evaluation Director described officers learning the data-input processes required under Section 119. This suggests that APPR preparation involves structured data collection and input, but it does not establish whether the process uses a DPLGA-provided digital platform, provincial spreadsheets, paper forms, or a mixture. [52]

Enga also illustrates an audit-oversight issue: its Provincial Administrator stated in 2025 that provincial audits had not been conducted for almost a decade, and called on DPLGA and the Auditor-General to reinstate regular audits. DPLGA’s Secretary responded that Section 119, Section 114, and Section 113 audit reports are constitutional requirements due before 30 April each year. [43]

**Relevance to PAIPS:** Enga demonstrates both active compliance activity and data-availability/verification constraints. It is a plausible interview target, but it is **not** a confirmed pilot partner.

### Western Highlands Provincial Administration

Western Highlands formally presented its 2024 Section 119 report to DPLGA in August 2025. The Provincial Administrator called for Section 114 and Section 119 reports to be presented together and urged DPLGA officials to physically verify implemented projects and programmes as part of monitoring and compliance. [44]

The report highlights infrastructure achievements, including maintenance of 49 strategic road links and 42 bridges. This demonstrates that provinces report substantive service-delivery and infrastructure information, but the public press release does not show the underlying data-collection method, indicator definitions, evidence standard, or digital system. [44]

**Relevance to PAIPS:** Western Highlands demonstrates that Section 119 reporting contains operational and service-delivery content. Its Administrator’s call for physical verification is direct evidence that reported results may require independent validation—a core PAIPS-relevant requirement.

### West New Britain Provincial Administration

West New Britain’s Corporate Plan 2023–2027, published by its Division of Planning, Coordination and Monitoring, sets out ten Key Result Areas and strategic measures for service delivery, budgeting and financial management, asset management, and legislative systems. [53]

This confirms that at least one provincial administration uses a formal corporate-planning framework with KRAs. However, the available public evidence does not show:

- A current digital departmental reporting system.
- A management dashboard.
- A public project-milestone tracker.
- A published APPR submission record.
- The province’s ICT readiness.

**Relevance to PAIPS:** West New Britain demonstrates the planning layer that PAIPS would need to align with, but it does not establish a current digital operational-reporting capability.

## 9. Confirmed gaps versus hypotheses

### Confirmed

1. **APPR and Section 119 reporting is an active statutory process.** Provinces submit reports; DPLGA compiles them for the Minister and Parliament. [43][52]

2. **Submission is incomplete.** DPLGA reported 14 of 20 provinces had submitted 2024 APPRs at the time of publication. [43]

3. **Data availability and verification are practical constraints.** Enga reported limited availability and verification of critical data; Western Highlands’ Administrator called for physical verification of projects. [44][52]

4. **Connectivity is a real deployment constraint.** The Department of Finance identifies internet interruptions and slow speeds outside Port Moresby as a concern for IFMS. [16]

5. **DPLGA already has a mandate for digital performance-reporting tools.** Any PAIPS proposal must engage DPLGA first to avoid duplication. [21]

### Partially confirmed

1. Departmental work plans and quarterly reporting exist as management practices, but digital tracking is not evidenced. [21][53]
2. Project and service-delivery reporting occurs through APPRs, but milestone-level tracking is not evidenced. [43][44][52]
3. National reporting exists, but the degree of digital consolidation is unknown. [43]

### Hypotheses

1. Provincial administrators lack timely consolidated operational visibility.
2. Departmental reports are manually consolidated.
3. Management actions are not systematically tracked to closure.
4. A dashboard would improve decision-making.
5. Officers would use a digital reporting tool if provided.

### Unknown

1. Whether DPLGA has an internal digital APPR platform.
2. Whether IFMS offers exports, reports, APIs, or approved data-sharing mechanisms.
3. Whether provinces already use departmental reporting tools not publicly documented.
4. Whether DPLGA publishes standard templates, indicators, or submission calendars.
5. Whether a province could fund and support a pilot.

| Commercial viability for supplier | Low | Medium | Uncertain | Medium if government-sponsored | Higher potential scale, but higher risk |

### Recommended option

**Option D is the least complex option that could address a demonstrated need**, but only after validation. It should add only capabilities not demonstrably covered by IFMS or DPLGA’s APPR process:

- Departmental input collection.
- Evidence attachment.
- Supervisor verification.
- Deadline and overdue tracking.
- APPR/Section 119 consolidation support.
- Management-action register.
- Authorised hierarchical views.

It should not duplicate IFMS financial processing, HR performance management, procurement, or national planning systems.

**Option B may become preferable** if DPLGA confirms that it already has, or is actively building, a suitable digital platform. **Option C and E should not proceed** before that confirmation.

## 11. Government adoption and procurement pathway

### Most appropriate first engagement

**DPLGA should be approached first**, specifically the Secretary’s Office, Corporate Planning and Performance Management Reporting Branch, and the Strategic Policy and Digital Integration Division.

Reason:

- DPLGA owns the APPR and Section 114/119 reporting process. [21]
- DPLGA is mandated to develop provincial performance-reporting systems. [21]
- DPLGA’s digital-integration unit is mandated to develop data-collection and performance-monitoring tools. [21]
- Engaging DPLGA first reduces the risk of building a duplicate system. [21]

### Provincial pilot without national deployment

A provincial pilot may be possible, but it should not be pursued independently of DPLGA. Because APPRs and Section 119 reports are ultimately submitted to DPLGA and compiled for Parliament, a province-level tool that does not align with DPLGA’s process could create another fragmented reporting channel. [43][52]

### Likely approvals and requirements

Before any software development, the following should be investigated:

1. **DPLGA approval** for workflow alignment, data definitions, and possible pilot participation.
2. **Department of Finance approval** for any IFMS data access, extract, report reuse, or integration.
3. **DICT alignment** with the Digital Government Plan, Government Technology Stack, data standards, cybersecurity, hosting, identity, and interoperability requirements. [1][4][6][18]
4. **Provincial administration approval** from the Provincial Administrator, with named departmental business owners.
5. **Procurement advice** from the National Procurement Commission or relevant provincial procurement authority.
6. **Legal and records advice** on data custodianship, retention, privacy, evidence, and audit requirements.

### Procurement status

No verified current tender specifically for a provincial performance-reporting or PAIPS-type platform was identified in the available public research. The National Procurement Commission has an active Electronic Government Procurement project, with an addendum extending the closing date in June 2026, but that procurement concerns e-GP, not provincial performance reporting. [47][51]

Therefore, **no procurement opportunity should be claimed**.

### Funding route

A province could potentially fund a pilot from an approved budget, but no evidence was found of an allocated provincial budget line for this purpose. Central sponsorship through DPLGA, Department of Finance, DICT, or a development partner may be more appropriate because the reporting process is national and statutory. This remains unresolved.

### Supplier readiness

A local PNG developer could legitimately offer implementation, configuration, training, support, and maintenance services, but it would need to demonstrate:

- PNG government procurement eligibility.
- ICT security and data-governance capability.
- Experience with government systems or comparable deployments.
- Local support capacity.
- Ability to work within DPLGA, Finance, and DICT requirements.
- Financial sustainability beyond initial development.
- References and verifiable delivery evidence.

### Risk of building before demand

Building a product before an expression of interest would create material risks:

- Duplication of DPLGA’s mandate or an undisclosed internal system. [21]
- Inability to integrate with IFMS. [16]
- Low adoption due to connectivity, staffing, and digital-literacy constraints. [16][40]
- No procurement pathway.
- High maintenance and support burden.
- Product mismatch with actual APPR templates and DPLGA requirements. [43][52]

## 12. Commercial feasibility assessment

### Public value

Public value is credible for a narrow system that improves APPR and Section 119 preparation, evidence verification, deadline compliance, and follow-up of corrective actions. Current evidence shows active reporting, incomplete submission, data-verification difficulty, and a stated need for physical verification. [43][44][52]

### Commercial opportunity

The commercial opportunity remains **unproven**.

There is no verified tender, budget line, approved vendor arrangement, or documented expression of interest for a provincial performance-reporting platform. The only directly relevant verified procurement found was the National Procurement Commission’s e-GP project, which is not a provincial performance-reporting procurement. [47][51]

The most realistic commercial route is:

1. DPLGA-led requirements definition.
2. A government-approved pilot in one willing province.
3. Independent evaluation against a baseline.
4. A decision by DPLGA, Finance, and DICT on national adoption.
5. A competitive procurement or framework arrangement for implementation and support.

A supplier should not develop a full product before at least a written expression of interest and a defined government business owner.

## Final recommendation

**Recommendation: D — Conduct interviews and validation before deciding.**

The evidence does not justify immediately building a new standalone PAIPS platform. DPLGA already owns the APPR and Section 114/119 reporting process and has an explicit mandate to develop provincial performance-reporting systems and digital data-collection tools. [21]

At the same time, abandonment is not justified. Current DPLGA reporting shows incomplete APPR submission, data-verification difficulty, and a stated need for physical verification of reported projects. IFMS covers financial management but does not publicly demonstrate non-financial work-plan, milestone, evidence, or management-action capability. [16][43][44][52]

The appropriate next step is a **DPLGA-led validation exercise**, followed—if justified—by a **small integration layer** for the APPR/Section 119 workflow. It should be built only after DPLGA, Department of Finance, DICT, and one willing provincial administration confirm the workflow, data sources, security pathway, and business ownership.

### Three most important unresolved questions

1. Does DPLGA already operate, or is it actively building, a digital APPR/Section 119 reporting platform?
2. Can IFMS provide authorised reports, extracts, or APIs for a provincial dashboard without duplicate financial data entry?
3. Which province has the management commitment, reporting baseline, connectivity, devices, and ICT support needed for a viable DPLGA-aligned pilot?
